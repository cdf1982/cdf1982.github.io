01Why Pingilla

One app instead of three.

For years I had a little app in my menu bar that told me when one of my websites went down. On the new macOS it started acting up, and an uptime monitor you can't trust is worse than no monitor at all, so I wrote my own.

While I was at it, I added the two things I kept opening other apps for: a scanner to see what's connected to my network, and a proper ping. The icon is Milla, my husky (glasses and bandana are artistic license). She notices anything that moves around the house; Pingilla tries to do the same with your sites and your network.

02Site Monitor

Know when a site goes down, and when it's back.

Add the websites you care about and Pingilla checks them in the background. When one stops answering you get a notification, and another one when it's back online, with how long it was down.

A red dot in the menu bar

While a site is down, the icon shows a red dot. Hover it to see which site and for how long, or click to see every site with its status, response time and last check.

No false alarms

Checks pause while your Mac sleeps or is offline. Before counting a failure, Pingilla also makes sure your Mac itself is online, so flaky Wi-Fi doesn't turn into a fake outage.

Uptime and incidents

Uptime and a response time chart over the last 24 hours, 7 days and 30 days, plus the list of incidents for each site: when it went down, for how long and why. With Pro, Pingilla also warns you two weeks before a site's SSL certificate expires. 7/30 DAYS AND CERTIFICATES: PRO

Slow is not down

Every site has its own slow threshold. A site that answers but takes too long turns yellow, without sending an alert for something that isn't an outage.

What the dots mean

  • UpThe site answered as expected.
  • SlowUp, but slower than the threshold you set for it.
  • FailingNot answering, but not for enough checks in a row to alert you yet.
  • DownFailed enough checks in a row: you got a notification.
  • WaitingNot checked yet.
03LAN Scanner

See every device on your network.

A scan takes about 10 seconds and lists what's connected: IP and MAC address, manufacturer (from an IEEE database that ships with the app, so no lookups online), names and ping time, plus services and open ports with Pro. Even iPhones and other devices that ignore ping show up. With Pro you can also open a device's web page, files, SSH or screen sharing in one click, and wake a sleeping Mac or NAS with Wake on LAN.

  1. 01Ping every addressA quick sweep of your whole local network.
  2. 02Knock on the quiet onesDevices that ignore ping still give themselves away on common ports.
  3. 03Read MAC addressesFrom your Mac's own table, to tell devices apart and find their maker.
  4. 04Names and servicesFrom Bonjour and DNS: AirPlay, file sharing, printers, HomeKit, SSH, web and more.
Remembers each network PRO

Home, office, your parents' house: Pingilla keeps the devices of each network separately, with when they were last seen, so you can tell what's new and what has gone missing.

Your names, notes and tags PRO

Call that mystery box "Living room speaker", write a note, tag devices (also many at once). Filter and sort the list, and choose which columns to see and in what order.

Scans on its own PRO

Turn on automatic scans for a network and Pingilla rescans it in the background every 15 or 30 minutes, 1 or 2 hours, only while your Mac is awake and on that network.

Honest results

Some Mac software, like DNS filters, answers certain ports for every address. Pingilla notices and leaves those results out, so a port shows as open only when it really is.

Tells you what changed PRO

With automatic scans on, you can get a notification when a new device joins your network. Mark the devices you care about as watched, and Pingilla tells you when one goes offline and when it comes back.

Fine with a VPN

With a VPN on, Pingilla still scans your local network, not the one at the other end of the tunnel, and still recognizes which network you're on.

04Ping

A real ping, one click away.

Type an IP address or a host name, local ones included, and Pingilla sends a real ICMP ping, like Terminal would. Paste a URL instead and it runs a series of web checks.

As many as you need

A short series, or keep going until you press Stop: handy while you move the router around the house. Open more Ping windows to watch several addresses side by side. UNLIMITED: PRO

The numbers that matter

Time and TTL for every reply, then packet loss and min/avg/max when the run ends.

Recent addresses

The addresses you pinged last wait in the menu bar menu, so pinging the NAS again is one click.

06Privacy

Your data stays on your Mac.

I don't want to know which sites you monitor or what's connected to your network, and Pingilla doesn't tell me. There's no account to create, no analytics, no tracking and no crash reporting service inside the app. Same rules in the free version and with Pro.

The only network traffic is what the features need:

  • Your sites, when Pingilla checks them.
  • Your local network, when you scan it or ping a device.
  • A quick connectivity check (Apple, then Google), only to confirm your Mac is online before calling a site down.
  • The App Store, if you buy Pingilla Pro.

Where your data lives:

  • Sites, uptime history, devices, names, notes and tags are saved in Pingilla's own folder on your Mac, and nowhere else.
  • There's no cloud sync and no server of mine in between.
  • Want a copy? Back up everything to a single file, export your devices and a site report as CSV, or print them and save as PDF, and keep them wherever you like.
  • The Privacy Policy and the Terms of Service are written by a human (me, Cesare) for other humans.

08Good to know

The small print, in plain words.

  • Pingilla is made for macOS 27 Golden Gate and also runs on macOS 26 Tahoe. It's available on the Mac App Store.
  • The first time you scan or ping a local device, macOS asks if Pingilla can access your local network: say yes, or it can't see your devices.
  • Uptime is what Pingilla actually observed: time your Mac spends asleep or offline doesn't count against a site.
  • Many phones and computers use private (randomized) MAC addresses, so their manufacturer can't be looked up; Pingilla labels them as private addresses.
  • Firewall apps that ask about new connections can hold the first packets for a moment, so the first pings to a new address may time out.
  • Nothing is deleted when you hit a free limit: extra sites are paused, and LAN data keeps being saved, ready when you upgrade.
